# Hat-in-Hand Asks

Small brands win supplier concessions by being easy to say yes to, not by pushing. Beverage, food and CPG are small worlds: today's supplier is tomorrow's introduction.

## Before drafting
1. **Get the ask from the user**, in their words: what they want (e.g. "25 kg first order instead of 80 kg", "hold last year's price", "Net 30") and their limit ("80 kg is fine if it can ship in splits"). If they haven't said, ask one line: *"What's the ask, and what would you settle for?"*
2. **Read the thread and the supplier's page** (if you keep notes per contact): what they already offered, their tone, anything they said they can't do. Don't re-ask something they already refused. Build on what they offered.
3. **Numbers come from the user or a quote on file** (`files/Finance/<supplier>/quote/`). If a number isn't in either place, it doesn't go in the email.

## The email (3–6 sentences)
1. **Warm, specific opener** about the real relationship ("Thanks again for turning the samples around so fast").
2. **Acknowledge their side** in one line ("I know small runs are a pain on your end").
3. **Ask a question before stating a position**, so they reveal their constraints ("Is there any flexibility on the first order size?").
4. **State the ask plainly**, once.
5. **Offer something real in return**, drawn only from what the user has said they can give: a rolling forecast, prepayment, a firm follow-on order, consolidated orders, faster payment.
6. **Easy next step**: "Happy to jump on a quick call if that's easier."

## By situation
| Ask | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Smaller first order / MOQ | "We're just getting started." Offer a firm follow-on or a forecast. Ask whether one run can be split into several shipments invoiced as they ship. |
| Price increase | Ask what's driving it and whether it applies to our spec before reacting. |
| Lower price | Ask whether there's a volume tier or a pack size that brings it down. |
| Payment terms | Tie it to growth and a track record of paying on time. Offer something in exchange. |
| Lead time | Get ahead of it: "Wanted to plan early rather than scramble." Ask what capacity looks like. |
| Quality problem | Flag it plainly and calmly. "Just want to understand what happened." Ask for their investigation and the COA or retain sample. No blame. |

## Never
- Mention another supplier, a competing quote or "we have options".
- Threaten, set deadlines, or say "final offer" unless the user wrote those words.
- Agree to anything, place an order, or accept terms on the user's behalf.
- Write more than six sentences, or use bullet lists in the email body.

## After drafting
Save it as a threaded draft (`threaded-replies`), label the thread `OfficerCan/NeedsYou`, and tell the user in one line what the draft asks for and what it offers. When the supplier answers, summarize their answer against the user's limit (inside it, outside it, or partly) and propose the next small step.
